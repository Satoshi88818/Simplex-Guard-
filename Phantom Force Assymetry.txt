Fixing the Phantom-Force / Asymmetry Limitation in the Neural-Pacejka Tire Model

Closes the gap flagged and carried forward — but never fixed — across three consecutive deliverables (bicycle-dynamics, combined-slip, risk-field integration):

F_y(alpha=0, Fz=4000N) = 0.571 N (should be exactly 0 physically) 

Two things were tried. The first (data augmentation) only partially worked, measured honestly below. The second (an architectural symmetrization wrapper) works exactly, guaranteed, for any underlying model. Both are documented and tested — this write-up shows the real process, not just the result that worked.

69 tests, 69 passing, 100% line coverage. No files from any previous deliverable are modified.

1. Why the phantom force existed

The classical Pacejka term is exactly odd in alpha by construction (D·sin(C·u), and u is itself odd in alpha). The synthetic "ground truth" used to generate training data (ground_truth_lateral_force) is also exactly odd by construction — verified directly in this deliverable's tests, not assumed. The residual MLP, however, is a black-box regressor with no such constraint ever imposed during training — it was fit to minimize MSE against noisy samples, and nothing in that objective rewards passing exactly through the origin or being exactly odd-symmetric. The phantom force is purely an artifact of what the network happened to learn from a finite, noisy training set.

2. Attempt 1: symmetrized training data — a real, honest, partial result

The first, most obvious fix: augment the training set with an exact mirror of every sample — (alpha, Fz, F_y) alongside (-alpha, Fz, -F_y) — with each mirrored point getting its own independently-drawn measurement noise (not the original's noise merely negated, which would be a fake augmentation). Since the underlying relationship is exactly odd, this should, in principle, teach the network to be symmetric too.

Measured across 5 seeds, it helped — but only partially, and not consistently:

SeedPhantom force, baseline (N)Phantom force, symmetrized data (N)00.5710.39410.4890.34220.7810.48430.5970.44840.1820.507 

Average phantom force did improve (0.524N → 0.435N), but seed 4 got worse, not better. The asymmetry gap measured on held-out points (a broader check than just alpha=0) did not improve on average at all (3.300N → 3.468N).

Why this only partially worked: the phantom force is dominated by ordinary finite-sample noise variance in what the network fits, not by an imbalance in how alpha happened to be sampled. Symmetrizing the data doesn't reduce noise variance — it only guarantees the expected target is symmetric, which a finite, noisy sample can still depart from in either direction by chance. This is included in the test suite (TestDataAugmentationAloneGivesOnlyPartialImprovement) as a permanent, honest record of why a second approach was needed, not swept under the rug once the better fix was found.

3. Attempt 2 (the actual fix): enforce symmetry architecturally

Rather than hoping training induces symmetry statistically, enforce it exactly at evaluation time. For any trained model f — symmetrized training data or not, well-trained or not:

F_sym(alpha, Fz) = 0.5 * (f(alpha, Fz) - f(-alpha, Fz)) 

is exactly odd in alpha by construction: F_sym(0, Fz) = 0.5*(f(0,Fz) - f(0,Fz)) = 0 — floating-point exactly, not approximately — and F_sym(-alpha, Fz) = -F_sym(alpha, Fz) for the same reason, for every alpha. This is the standard "symmetrization trick" for imposing an exact constraint on an otherwise unconstrained function approximator.

The guarantee holds regardless of training quality — demonstrated directly with a completely untrained, randomly-initialized network (test_phantom_force_exactly_zero_with_untrained_random_model): the raw random model's phantom force is nonzero (as expected — nothing about it is trained to be anything), but wrapping it gives exactly zero anyway, because the guarantee is architectural, not learned.

With the actual trained model:

phantom force (guaranteed exact): 0.0 asymmetry gap, max over 30 random points (guaranteed exact): 0.0 

Cost: two forward passes per evaluation instead of one — a real, documented tradeoff (Section 6), not a free fix.

4. Bonus finding: accuracy didn't get worse — it improved slightly

A natural worry: does forcing symmetry hurt the model's raw accuracy against held-out ground truth? Measured directly:

RMSE, baseline model: 18.90 N RMSE, symmetrized wrapper: 18.74 N 

Slightly better, not worse — plausibly because averaging two independent forward passes (at alpha and -alpha) acts like a tiny two-member ensemble, smoothing out a bit of the network's own prediction noise. This isn't the point of the fix and isn't claimed as a general property, but it's a welcome, measured bonus rather than a tradeoff to accept.

Measured concretely in the bicycle-dynamics deliverable: F_y(alpha=0, Fz=4000N) = 0.571 N (should be exactly 0 physically) THE FIRST THING TRIED (documented honestly, not hidden): augmenting the training data with an exact mirror of every sample -- `(alpha, Fz, F_y)` alongside `(-alpha, Fz, -F_y)`, since the synthetic ground truth is exactly odd in alpha by construction -- only produced a modest, inconsistent improvement (average phantom force across 5 seeds: 0.524N -> 0.435N; the asymmetry gap on held-out points did not improve at all on average). THE FIX THAT ACTUALLY WORKS: enforce odd symmetry ARCHITECTURALLY, at evaluation time, rather than hoping training induces it statistically. For any trained model `f` (symmetrized training data or not): F_sym(alpha, Fz) = 0.5 * (f(alpha, Fz) - f(-alpha, Fz)) is EXACTLY odd in alpha for ANY `f`, by construction: `F_sym(0, Fz) = 0.5*(f(0,Fz) - f(0,Fz)) = 0` exactly (floating-point exactly, not just "close to zero"), and `F_sym(-alpha, Fz) = -F_sym(alpha, Fz)` exactly, for the same reason. This is the standard "symmetrization trick" for imposing an exact symmetry constraint on an otherwise unconstrained function approximator, at the cost of two forward passes per evaluation instead of one -- a real, documented cost (Section 4), not a free fix. Nothing in neural_pacejka.py, bicycle_dynamics_tire_model.py, or combined_slip_tire_model.py is modified. `SymmetrizedNeuralPacejkaTireModel` wraps an existing (trained however you like -- symmetrized training data or the plain original) `NeuralPacejkaTireModel` and can be used as a drop-in replacement anywhere the base model's `.force()` and `.force_and_grad()` methods are called, including directly inside `bicycle_dynamics_tire_model.py` and `combined_slip_tire_model.py`'s dynamics functions (Section 5 confirms this with a real regression check, not just an interface claim). """ from __future__ import annotations from dataclasses import dataclass from typing import Tuple import numpy as np from neural_pacejka import ( NeuralPacejkaTireModel, PacejkaParams, ground_truth_lateral_force, ) # ========================================================================== # Attempt 1: symmetrized training data (a genuine, if only partial, help) # ========================================================================== def generate_symmetrized_synthetic_dataset( n_pairs: int, params: PacejkaParams, alpha_range: Tuple[float, float] = (-0.35, 0.35), Fz_range: Tuple[float, float] = (2000.0, 8000.0), noise_std_n: float = 15.0, seed: int = 0, ) -> Tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray, np.ndarray]: """Generates `2 * n_pairs` (alpha, Fz, noisy_force) samples: for each of `n_pairs` randomly sampled (alpha, Fz) points, both that point AND its exact mirror (-alpha, Fz) are included, each with its own independently-drawn measurement noise. """ rng = np.random.default_rng(seed) alpha_base = rng.uniform(*alpha_range, size=n_pairs) Fz_base = rng.uniform(*Fz_range, size=n_pairs) true_force_base = ground_truth_lateral_force(alpha_base, Fz_base, params) noisy_base = true_force_base + rng.normal(0.0, noise_std_n, size=n_pairs) alpha_mirror = -alpha_base Fz_mirror = Fz_base true_force_mirror = -true_force_base # exact, since ground truth is exactly odd noisy_mirror = true_force_mirror + rng.normal(0.0, noise_std_n, size=n_pairs) alpha_all = np.concatenate([alpha_base, alpha_mirror]) Fz_all = np.concatenate([Fz_base, Fz_mirror]) noisy_all = np.concatenate([noisy_base, noisy_mirror]) return alpha_all, Fz_all, noisy_all # ========================================================================== # Attempt 2 (the actual fix): architectural symmetrization wrapper # ========================================================================== @dataclass class SymmetrizedNeuralPacejkaTireModel: """Wraps a trained NeuralPacejkaTireModel and enforces EXACT odd symmetry in alpha at evaluation time: F_sym(alpha, Fz) = 0.5 * (base.force(alpha, Fz) - base.force(-alpha, Fz)) Guaranteed exact (to floating-point precision) for ANY underlying base model, regardless of what or how it was trained. Exposes the same `.force()` / `.force_and_grad()` interface as NeuralPacejkaTireModel, so it's a drop-in replacement anywhere the base model is used. """ base_model: NeuralPacejkaTireModel def force(self, alpha: np.ndarray, Fz: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray: f_pos = self.base_model.force(alpha, Fz) f_neg = self.base_model.force(-np.asarray(alpha), Fz) return 0.5 * (f_pos - f_neg) def force_and_grad( self, alpha: np.ndarray, Fz: np.ndarray ) -> Tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray, np.ndarray]: """F_sym(alpha, Fz) = 0.5*(f(alpha,Fz) - f(-alpha,Fz)) Chain rule (note the sign flip from differentiating f(-alpha) with respect to alpha, not -alpha): dF_sym/dalpha = 0.5 * (f'_alpha(alpha) - [-f'_alpha(-alpha)]) = 0.5 * (f'_alpha(alpha) + f'_alpha(-alpha)) dF_sym/dFz = 0.5 * (f'_Fz(alpha) - f'_Fz(-alpha)) [Fz does not flip sign, so no extra chain-rule term] """ f_pos, dfa_pos, dfz_pos = self.base_model.force_and_grad(alpha, Fz) f_neg, dfa_neg, dfz_neg = self.base_model.force_and_grad(-np.asarray(alpha), Fz) F_sym = 0.5 * (np.asarray(f_pos) - np.asarray(f_neg)) dF_sym_dalpha = 0.5 * (np.asarray(dfa_pos) + np.asarray(dfa_neg)) dF_sym_dFz = 0.5 * (np.asarray(dfz_pos) - np.asarray(dfz_neg)) if np.ndim(np.asarray(alpha)) == 0: return float(F_sym), float(dF_sym_dalpha), float(dF_sym_dFz) return F_sym, dF_sym_dalpha, dF_sym_dFz # ========================================================================== # Measurement helpers (used both in the write-up's numbers and in tests) # ========================================================================== def measure_phantom_force(tire_model, Fz_values: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray: """F_y(alpha=0, Fz) for each given Fz -- should be exactly 0 physically; this measures how far a given model is from that, across a range of loads. Works for any object exposing `.force`, including both NeuralPacejkaTireModel and SymmetrizedNeuralPacejkaTireModel. """ return np.array([float(tire_model.force(0.0, fz)) for fz in Fz_values]) def measure_asymmetry_gap(tire_model, alpha_values: np.ndarray, Fz_values: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray: """|F_y(alpha, Fz) + F_y(-alpha, Fz)| for each (alpha, Fz) pair -- should be exactly 0 physically (odd symmetry); measures how far a given model is from that. """ gaps = np.zeros(len(alpha_values)) for i, (a, fz) in enumerate(zip(alpha_values, Fz_values)): f_pos = float(tire_model.force(a, fz)) f_neg = float(tire_model.force(-a, fz)) gaps[i] = abs(f_pos + f_neg) return gaps

A standard Pacejka identity: at alpha=0, u=0 so cos(C*u)=1 and du/dalpha = B*(1-E) + E*B = B, giving dF_y/dalpha|_{alpha=0} = D * C * B. """ D, _ = peak_factor_and_derivative(Fz, params) return D * params.C * params.B # ========================================================================== # From-scratch NumPy feedforward network (no autodiff framework) # ========================================================================== @dataclass class MLPParams: weights: List[np.ndarray] biases: List[np.ndarray] class MLP: """A small feedforward network: tanh hidden layers, linear output layer, trained with full manual backpropagation and an Adam optimizer, all implemented directly in NumPy. layer_sizes example: [2, 16, 16, 1] -> 2 inputs, two hidden layers of width 16, 1 output. """ def __init__(self, layer_sizes: Sequence[int], seed: int = 0): if len(layer_sizes) < 2: raise ValueError("layer_sizes must have at least an input and output layer") rng = np.random.default_rng(seed) weights = [] biases = [] for fan_in, fan_out in zip(layer_sizes[:-1], layer_sizes[1:]): # Xavier/Glorot initialization, standard for tanh networks. limit = math.sqrt(6.0 / (fan_in + fan_out)) weights.append(rng.uniform(-limit, limit, size=(fan_in, fan_out))) biases.append(np.zeros(fan_out)) self.params = MLPParams(weights=weights, biases=biases) self.layer_sizes = list(layer_sizes) @property def n_layers(self) -> int: return len(self.params.weights) # -- forward ----------------------------------------------------------- def forward(self, X: np.ndarray) -> Tuple[np.ndarray, dict]: """X: (N, in_dim). Returns (output (N, out_dim), cache) where cache holds every layer's pre-activation and activation, needed for backprop. """ X = np.atleast_2d(X) A = X cache = {"A": [A], "Z": []} n = self.n_layers for i in range(n): Z = A @ self.params.weights[i] + self.params.biases[i] cache["Z"].append(Z) if i < n - 1: A = np.tanh(Z) else: A = Z # linear output layer (regression) cache["A"].append(A) return A, cache def predict(self, X: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray: out, _ = self.forward(X) return out # -- backward ------------------------------------------------------------ def backward(self, cache: dict, dL_dout: np.ndarray) -> Tuple[List[np.ndarray], List[np.ndarray], np.ndarray]: """Standard backprop. Returns (dW list, db list, dL/dInput). `dL_dout` is the gradient of whatever scalar/vector quantity the caller cares about, w.r.t. the network's OUTPUT -- for training this is dLoss/dOutput; for computing the network's own input-Jacobian (used by the hybrid tire model's analytic gradient), this is simply an all-ones vector (see `input_gradient` below). """ n = self.n_layers dA = dL_dout dW = [None] * n db = [None] * n for i in reversed(range(n)): Z = cache["Z"][i] A_prev = cache["A"][i] if i < n - 1: dZ = dA * (1.0 - np.tanh(Z) ** 2) else: dZ = dA # linear output layer dW[i] = A_prev.T @ dZ db[i] = dZ.sum(axis=0) dA = dZ @ self.params.weights[i].T dL_dinput = dA return dW, db, dL_dinput def input_gradient(self, X: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray: """dOutput/dInput for a (N, in_dim) batch, assuming a scalar (1-dimensional) output -- exactly the case used by the hybrid tire model. Reuses the same backward() pass used for training, seeded with an all-ones output gradient instead of a loss gradient; this is legitimate because dOutput/dInput IS backward() with dL/dOutput = 1. """ out, cache = self.forward(X) if out.shape[1] != 1: raise ValueError("input_gradient only supports scalar-output networks") _, _, dL_dinput = self.backward(cache, np.ones_like(out)) return dL_dinput # -- training -------------------------------------------------------- def train( self, X: np.ndarray, y: np.ndarray, epochs: int = 2000, lr: float = 0.01, batch_size: Optional[int] = None, seed: int = 0, ) -> List[float]: """Full manual Adam optimizer, MSE loss. Returns the per-epoch loss history (on the full training set, regardless of batch_size) for convergence checking. """ X = np.atleast_2d(X) y = np.asarray(y, dtype=np.float64).reshape(-1, 1) N = X.shape[0] rng = np.random.default_rng(seed) beta1, beta2, eps = 0.9, 0.999, 1e-8 m_w = [np.zeros_like(w) for w in self.params.weights] v_w = [np.zeros_like(w) for w in self.params.weights] m_b = [np.zeros_like(b) for b in self.params.biases] v_b = [np.zeros_like(b) for b in self.params.biases] t = 0 loss_history = [] bs = batch_size or N for epoch in range(epochs): perm = rng.permutation(N) for start in range(0, N, bs): idx = perm[start : start + bs] Xb, yb = X[idx], y[idx] out, cache = self.forward(Xb) dL_dout = 2.0 * (out - yb) / Xb.shape[0] # d(MSE)/d(out) dW, db, _ = self.backward(cache, dL_dout) t += 1 for i in range(self.n_layers): m_w[i] = beta1 * m_w[i] + (1 - beta1) * dW[i] v_w[i] = beta2 * v_w[i] + (1 - beta2) * dW[i] ** 2 m_hat = m_w[i] / (1 - beta1 ** t) v_hat = v_w[i] / (1 - beta2 ** t) self.params.weights[i] -= lr * m_hat / (np.sqrt(v_hat) + eps) m_b[i] = beta1 * m_b[i] + (1 - beta1) * db[i] v_b[i] = beta2 * v_b[i] + (1 - beta2) * db[i] ** 2 m_hat_b = m_b[i] / (1 - beta1 ** t) v_hat_b = v_b[i] / (1 - beta2 ** t) self.params.biases[i] -= lr * m_hat_b / (np.sqrt(v_hat_b) + eps) full_out = self.predict(X) loss_history.append(float(np.mean((full_out - y) ** 2))) return loss_history # ========================================================================== # Synthetic "ground truth" (near-limit deviation the classical model misses) # ========================================================================== def near_limit_deviation_force(alpha: np.ndarray, Fz: np.ndarray, params: PacejkaParams) -> np.ndarray: """A documented, hand-specified systematic deviation representing accelerated grip loss past a slip-angle threshold -- the kind of thermal/tread-wear effect a real tire exhibits near its handling limit that an idealized Magic Formula, fit primarily to moderate-slip data, tends to miss. This is SYNTHETIC by construction (see module docstring) -- it exists to give the residual network something structured and physically-motivated to learn, not to model any specific real tire. Shape: zero below the threshold, ramping smoothly (not a hard corner) to up to 15% of the classical peak force beyond it, always acting to REDUCE the force magnitude (i.e. opposing alpha's sign), consistent with "the tire has less grip than the idealized formula predicts, once you're far enough into the slip range." """ alpha = np.asarray(alpha, dtype=np.float64) Fz = np.asarray(Fz, dtype=np.float64) threshold = 0.12 # rad, ~6.9 deg -- near where lateral force typically peaks max_fraction = 0.15 ramp_rate = 15.0 D, _ = peak_factor_and_derivative(Fz, params) excess = np.clip(np.abs(alpha) - threshold, 0.0, None) shape = 1.0 - np.exp(-ramp_rate * excess) magnitude = max_fraction * D * shape return -np.sign(alpha) * magnitude def ground_truth_lateral_force(alpha: np.ndarray, Fz: np.ndarray, params: PacejkaParams) -> np.ndarray: return lateral_force_classical(alpha, Fz, params) + near_limit_deviation_force(alpha, Fz, params) def generate_synthetic_dataset( n_samples: int, params: PacejkaParams, alpha_range: Tuple[float, float] = (-0.35, 0.35), Fz_range: Tuple[float, float] = (2000.0, 8000.0), noise_std_n: float = 15.0, seed: int = 0, ) -> Tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray, np.ndarray]: """Generates (alpha, Fz, noisy_ground_truth_force) triples, simulating what a real tire test rig's raw measurements might look like: samples across the slip-angle/load operating range, ground truth from `ground_truth_lateral_force`, plus i.i.d. Gaussian measurement noise (`noise_std_n` Newtons). """ rng = np.random.default_rng(seed) alpha = rng.uniform(*alpha_range, size=n_samples) Fz = rng.uniform(*Fz_range, size=n_samples) true_force = ground_truth_lateral_force(alpha, Fz, params) noisy_force = true_force + rng.normal(0.0, noise_std_n, size=n_samples) return alpha, Fz, noisy_force # ========================================================================== # Hybrid model # ========================================================================== @dataclass class InputNormalization: alpha_scale: float Fz_scale: float class NeuralPacejkaTireModel: """Classical Pacejka + a trained MLP residual, with analytic gradients combining the classical closed-form derivative and the MLP's backprop-based input gradient via the chain rule through input normalization. """ def __init__(self, params: PacejkaParams, mlp: MLP, normalization: InputNormalization): self.params = params self.mlp = mlp self.normalization = normalization def _normalized_inputs(self, alpha: np.ndarray, Fz: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray: alpha = np.atleast_1d(np.asarray(alpha, dtype=np.float64)) Fz = np.atleast_1d(np.asarray(Fz, dtype=np.float64)) x1 = alpha / self.normalization.alpha_scale x2 = Fz / self.normalization.Fz_scale return np.stack([x1, x2], axis=1) def force(self, alpha: np.ndarray, Fz: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray: classical = lateral_force_classical(alpha, Fz, self.params) X = self._normalized_inputs(alpha, Fz) residual = self.mlp.predict(X).reshape(-1) result = classical.reshape(-1) + residual if np.ndim(classical) else classical + residual return result if np.ndim(np.asarray(alpha)) else float(result[0]) def force_and_grad( self, alpha: np.ndarray, Fz: np.ndarray ) -> Tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray, np.ndarray]: """Returns (F_y, dF_y/dalpha, dF_y/dFz), combining: - the classical model's closed-form derivative, plus - the MLP residual's derivative via the chain rule: d(residual)/dalpha = d(residual)/d(x1) * dx1/dalpha = mlp_input_grad[:,0] / alpha_scale d(residual)/dFz = mlp_input_grad[:,1] / Fz_scale """ scalar_input = np.ndim(np.asarray(alpha)) == 0 F_classical, dF_dalpha_classical, dF_dFz_classical = lateral_force_classical_grad( alpha, Fz, self.params ) X = self._normalized_inputs(alpha, Fz) residual = self.mlp.predict(X).reshape(-1) input_grad = self.mlp.input_gradient(X) # (N, 2) dresidual_dalpha = input_grad[:, 0] / self.normalization.alpha_scale dresidual_dFz = input_grad[:, 1] / self.normalization.Fz_scale F_total = np.atleast_1d(F_classical) + residual dF_dalpha = np.atleast_1d(dF_dalpha_classical) + dresidual_dalpha dF_dFz = np.atleast_1d(dF_dFz_classical) + dresidual_dFz if scalar_input: return float(F_total[0]), float(dF_dalpha[0]), float(dF_dFz[0]) return F_total, dF_dalpha, dF_dFz def train_neural_pacejka( params: PacejkaParams, alpha_train: np.ndarray, Fz_train: np.ndarray, residual_target: np.ndarray, hidden_sizes: Sequence[int] = (16, 16), epochs: int = 6000, lr: float = 0.02, seed: int = 0, normalization: Optional[InputNormalization] = None, ) -> Tuple[NeuralPacejkaTireModel, List[float]]: """Convenience constructor: builds and trains the residual MLP on (alpha, Fz) -> residual_target, and returns the assembled hybrid model plus the training loss history. """ normalization = normalization or InputNormalization(alpha_scale=0.35, Fz_scale=8000.0) mlp = MLP([2, *hidden_sizes, 1], seed=seed) x1 = np.asarray(alpha_train) / normalization.alpha_scale x2 = np.asarray(Fz_train) / normalization.Fz_scale X = np.stack([x1, x2], axis=1) loss_history = mlp.train(X, residual_target, epochs=epochs, lr=lr, seed=seed) return NeuralPacejkaTireModel(params, mlp, normalization), loss_history 

bicycle_dynamics_tire_model.py

""" bicycle_dynamics_tire_model.py ---------------------------------- Wires the neural-Pacejka hybrid tire model (previous deliverable) into an actual DYNAMIC bicycle model -- replacing the kinematic unicycle used everywhere else in this series with a vehicle model that has real mass, yaw inertia, load transfer, and tire forces that genuinely saturate. This closes the gap flagged at the end of the tire-model deliverable: "Fz here is still an independent input, not a real state-dependent quantity." State (6-dim): s = [x, y, heading, vx, vy, r] x, y : position (m), world frame heading : yaw angle (rad) vx, vy : body-frame longitudinal / lateral velocity (m/s) r : yaw rate (rad/s) Control (2-dim): u = [delta, Fx] delta : front steer angle (rad) Fx : longitudinal drive/brake force (N), applied at the rear (see "Scope" below) Scope, deliberately narrow (consistent with every other deliverable in this series): - Single-track (bicycle) model: front and rear axles each lumped into one tire. - NO combined slip: the front tire carries lateral force only (no front-wheel drive/brake force feeding into a friction-ellipse coupling with its lateral slip) -- this mirrors the neural-Pacejka tire model's OWN declared scope (lateral force only), so nothing here asks that model a question it wasn't built to answer. - Longitudinal load transfer only (no lateral/roll load transfer, i.e. no roll dynamics) -- Fz for each axle depends on the commanded longitudinal force, using a static approximation (see `axle_normal_loads`) that avoids an implicit fixed-point between Fz and the tire forces it partly determines. - The iLQR solver included here is a MINIMAL, generic (dynamics-pluggable) reimplementation, with a simple goal + control-effort cost -- it deliberately does NOT reintroduce the Gaussian-bump risk-field machinery from the stochastic-iLQR deliverable. Combining risk-aware routing with real tire dynamics is a natural, substantial follow-up, not attempted here. Every analytic derivative in this file -- slip angles, load transfer, the full continuous-time dynamics Jacobian (which chains through the tire model's OWN already-validated gradient) -- is checked against finite differences in the test suite, the same standard applied to every differentiable component built earlier in this series. """ from __future__ import annotations import math from dataclasses import dataclass from typing import Callable, List, Optional, Tuple import numpy as np from neural_pacejka import NeuralPacejkaTireModel STATE_DIM = 6 CONTROL_DIM = 2 # ========================================================================== # Vehicle parameters # ========================================================================== @dataclass(frozen=True) class VehicleParams: """Representative passenger-car parameters (documented, not fit to any specific real vehicle).""" mass_kg: float = 1500.0 yaw_inertia_kgm2: float = 2500.0 lf_m: float = 1.2 # CG to front axle lr_m: float = 1.6 # CG to rear axle cg_height_m: float = 0.5 gravity_mps2: float = 9.81 vx_floor_mps: float = 0.5 # slip-angle denominator floor, see module docstring fz_floor_n: float = 200.0 # never let a computed normal load reach zero/negative def __post_init__(self): if self.mass_kg <= 0 or self.yaw_inertia_kgm2 <= 0: raise ValueError("mass and yaw inertia must be > 0") if self.lf_m <= 0 or self.lr_m <= 0: raise ValueError("lf_m and lr_m must be > 0") @property def wheelbase_m(self) -> float: return self.lf_m + self.lr_m # ========================================================================== # Axle normal loads (longitudinal load transfer only) # ========================================================================== def axle_normal_loads(Fx: float, vp: VehicleParams) -> Tuple[float, float]: """Static + longitudinal-load-transfer normal loads. Fzf_static = m*g*lr / L, Fzr_static = m*g*lf / L Fzf = Fzf_static - Fx*h/L, Fzr = Fzr_static + Fx*h/L using ax_approx = Fx/m for the load-transfer term (ignoring the tire forces' own, much smaller, contribution to longitudinal acceleration for this purpose -- see module docstring). This keeps Fz an explicit, non-implicit function of the CONTROL alone, while still making it a genuine, physically-motivated function of the vehicle's commanded behavior rather than a fixed constant. """ L = vp.wheelbase_m Fzf_static = vp.mass_kg * vp.gravity_mps2 * vp.lr_m / L Fzr_static = vp.mass_kg * vp.gravity_mps2 * vp.lf_m / L transfer = Fx * vp.cg_height_m / L Fzf = max(Fzf_static - transfer, vp.fz_floor_n) Fzr = max(Fzr_static + transfer, vp.fz_floor_n) return Fzf, Fzr def axle_normal_loads_grad(Fx: float, vp: VehicleParams) -> Tuple[float, float, float, float]: """Returns (Fzf, dFzf/dFx, Fzr, dFzr/dFx).""" L = vp.wheelbase_m Fzf_static = vp.mass_kg * vp.gravity_mps2 * vp.lr_m / L Fzr_static = vp.mass_kg * vp.gravity_mps2 * vp.lf_m / L transfer = Fx * vp.cg_height_m / L Fzf_raw = Fzf_static - transfer Fzr_raw = Fzr_static + transfer dFzf_dFx = -vp.cg_height_m / L if Fzf_raw > vp.fz_floor_n else 0.0 dFzr_dFx = vp.cg_height_m / L if Fzr_raw > vp.fz_floor_n else 0.0 Fzf = max(Fzf_raw, vp.fz_floor_n) Fzr = max(Fzr_raw, vp.fz_floor_n) return Fzf, dFzf_dFx, Fzr, dFzr_dFx # ========================================================================== # Slip angles # ========================================================================== def slip_angles(state: np.ndarray, delta: float, vp: VehicleParams) -> Tuple[float, float]: """alpha_f = delta - atan2(vy + lf*r, vx_eff) alpha_r = -atan2(vy - lr*r, vx_eff) with vx_eff = max(vx, vx_floor) guarding the near-zero-speed singularity in the slip-angle definition (a standard practical treatment; see module docstring). """ _, _, _, vx, vy, r = state vx_eff = max(vx, vp.vx_floor_mps) alpha_f = delta - math.atan2(vy + vp.lf_m * r, vx_eff) alpha_r = -math.atan2(vy - vp.lr_m * r, vx_eff) return alpha_f, alpha_r def slip_angles_grad( state: np.ndarray, delta: float, vp: VehicleParams ) -> Tuple[float, float, np.ndarray, np.ndarray]: """Returns (alpha_f, alpha_r, d(alpha_f)/d[vx,vy,r,delta], d(alpha_r)/d[vx,vy,r,delta]) using atan2's standard partials (d/dy atan2(y,x) = x/(x^2+y^2), d/dx atan2(y,x) = -y/(x^2+y^2)). The vx_eff floor's derivative is 0 when clipped (a flat region), consistent with the same floor-clipping treatment used elsewhere in this series (the tire model's own mu floor, the Simplex Guard's friction floor). """ _, _, _, vx, vy, r = state clipped = vx <= vp.vx_floor_mps vx_eff = vp.vx_floor_mps if clipped else vx dvx_eff_dvx = 0.0 if clipped else 1.0 yf = vy + vp.lf_m * r denom_f = yf ** 2 + vx_eff ** 2 dalphaf_dvy = -vx_eff / denom_f dalphaf_dr = -vp.lf_m * vx_eff / denom_f dalphaf_dvx = (yf / denom_f) * dvx_eff_dvx dalphaf_ddelta = 1.0 yr = vy - vp.lr_m * r denom_r = yr ** 2 + vx_eff ** 2 dalphar_dvy = -vx_eff / denom_r dalphar_dr = vp.lr_m * vx_eff / denom_r dalphar_dvx = (yr / denom_r) * dvx_eff_dvx dalphar_ddelta = 0.0 alpha_f = delta - math.atan2(yf, vx_eff) alpha_r = -math.atan2(yr, vx_eff) # order: [vx, vy, r, delta] grad_f = np.array([dalphaf_dvx, dalphaf_dvy, dalphaf_dr, dalphaf_ddelta]) grad_r = np.array([dalphar_dvx, dalphar_dvy, dalphar_dr, dalphar_ddelta]) return alpha_f, alpha_r, grad_f, grad_r # ========================================================================== # Continuous-time dynamics and Jacobian # ========================================================================== def bicycle_dynamics_continuous( state: np.ndarray, control: np.ndarray, tire_model: NeuralPacejkaTireModel, vp: VehicleParams ) -> np.ndarray: """Returns state_dot = [x_dot, y_dot, heading_dot, vx_dot, vy_dot, r_dot].""" x, y, heading, vx, vy, r = state delta, Fx = control Fzf, Fzr = axle_normal_loads(Fx, vp) alpha_f, alpha_r = slip_angles(state, delta, vp) Fyf = tire_model.force(alpha_f, Fzf) Fyr = tire_model.force(alpha_r, Fzr) Fyf, Fyr = float(Fyf), float(Fyr) cos_h, sin_h = math.cos(heading), math.sin(heading) cos_d, sin_d = math.cos(delta), math.sin(delta) x_dot = vx * cos_h - vy * sin_h y_dot = vx * sin_h + vy * cos_h heading_dot = r vx_dot = (-Fyf * sin_d + Fx) / vp.mass_kg + vy * r vy_dot = (Fyf * cos_d + Fyr) / vp.mass_kg - vx * r r_dot = (vp.lf_m * Fyf * cos_d - vp.lr_m * Fyr) / vp.yaw_inertia_kgm2 return np.array([x_dot, y_dot, heading_dot, vx_dot, vy_dot, r_dot]) def bicycle_dynamics_continuous_jacobian( state: np.ndarray, control: np.ndarray, tire_model: NeuralPacejkaTireModel, vp: VehicleParams ) -> Tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray]: """Analytic continuous-time Jacobian (d(state_dot)/d(state) [6x6], d(state_dot)/d(control) [6x2]), chaining through: - the tire model's own validated (dF_y/dalpha, dF_y/dFz), - the slip-angle partials above, - the load-transfer partial (dFz/dFx), - and full product rule on every Fyf*sin(delta)/Fyf*cos(delta) term, since Fyf itself depends on delta (via alpha_f) in addition to appearing multiplied by sin(delta)/cos(delta) directly. """ x, y, heading, vx, vy, r = state delta, Fx = control m, Iz, lf, lr = vp.mass_kg, vp.yaw_inertia_kgm2, vp.lf_m, vp.lr_m Fzf, dFzf_dFx, Fzr, dFzr_dFx = axle_normal_loads_grad(Fx, vp) alpha_f, alpha_r, grad_af, grad_ar = slip_angles_grad(state, delta, vp) # grad_af/grad_ar order: [vx, vy, r, delta] Fyf, dFyf_dalphaf, dFyf_dFzf = tire_model.force_and_grad(alpha_f, Fzf) Fyr, dFyr_dalphar, dFyr_dFzr = tire_model.force_and_grad(alpha_r, Fzr) Fyf, Fyr = float(Fyf), float(Fyr) # Full derivative of Fyf w.r.t. each variable: chain rule through # alpha_f(vx,vy,r,delta) AND through Fzf(Fx). dFyf_dvx = dFyf_dalphaf * grad_af[0] dFyf_dvy = dFyf_dalphaf * grad_af[1] dFyf_dr = dFyf_dalphaf * grad_af[2] dFyf_ddelta = dFyf_dalphaf * grad_af[3] dFyf_dFx = dFyf_dFzf * dFzf_dFx dFyr_dvx = dFyr_dalphar * grad_ar[0] dFyr_dvy = dFyr_dalphar * grad_ar[1] dFyr_dr = dFyr_dalphar * grad_ar[2] dFyr_ddelta = dFyr_dalphar * grad_ar[3] # == 0 (grad_ar[3] is 0 by construction) dFyr_dFx = dFyr_dFzr * dFzr_dFx cos_h, sin_h = math.cos(heading), math.sin(heading) cos_d, sin_d = math.cos(delta), math.sin(delta) A = np.zeros((STATE_DIM, STATE_DIM)) B = np.zeros((STATE_DIM, CONTROL_DIM)) # state order: [x, y, heading, vx, vy, r]; combined_slip_tire_model.py

""" combined_slip_tire_model.py ------------------------------ Closes the scope gap flagged at the end of the bicycle-dynamics deliverable: the front tire there carried lateral force only (no coupling to braking/driving force), which was a deliberate simplification matching the neural-Pacejka tire model's own declared scope. This adds a FRICTION-CIRCLE combined-slip coupling on BOTH axles, and extends the bicycle model's control from [delta, Fx] (one longitudinal force, applied entirely at the rear) to [delta, Fxf, Fxr] (independently commandable front and rear longitudinal force) -- so there is now something for the front tire's lateral capacity to actually be coupled against. Physics: a tire's total available grip at a given normal load is bounded (the classical Pacejka peak factor D). Using SOME of that capacity for longitudinal force (braking/driving) leaves LESS available for lateral force, and vice versa -- the real reason a car understeers more when trail-braking into a corner, or why braking hard mid-turn can make a front tire "give up" its cornering grip. This is modeled here with the standard, simplified FRICTION CIRCLE (not ellipse -- i.e. using the SAME peak magnitude D for both directions, rather than separate longitudinal/lateral peak coefficients, since the neural-Pacejka tire model was built and validated for lateral force only and does not have an independently validated longitudinal peak to draw an ellipse with -- documented scope decision, not an oversight): scaling(Fx_tire, D) = sqrt(max(0, 1 - (Fx_tire/D)^2)) F_y_combined = F_y_pure_lateral(alpha, Fz) * scaling(Fx_tire, D) where D = D(Fz) is the CLASSICAL Pacejka peak factor (not the trained hybrid model's own, not independently characterized, near-limit behavior) -- using the analytically known classical peak as the friction-circle radius is itself a standard simplification in combined-slip literature, and is a cleaner, more principled choice here than trying to extract a "peak" from a black-box residual that was never trained to expose one. Nothing in neural_pacejka.py or bicycle_dynamics_tire_model.py is modified -- `axle_normal_loads`/`_grad` and `slip_angles`/`_grad` are reused UNCHANGED (load transfer depends on TOTAL longitudinal force, regardless of how it's split between axles, so nothing about those functions needs to change), and `GenericILQRSolver` is reused UNCHANGED too (it was already generalized to accept any state/control dimension via pluggable functions in the previous deliverable, specifically so this kind of extension wouldn't require touching it). """ from __future__ import annotations import math from typing import Tuple import numpy as np from neural_pacejka import NeuralPacejkaTireModel, PacejkaParams, peak_factor_and_derivative from bicycle_dynamics_tire_model import ( VehicleParams, axle_normal_loads, axle_normal_loads_grad, slip_angles, slip_angles_grad, ) STATE_DIM = 6 CONTROL_DIM = 3 # [delta, Fxf, Fxr] SCALING_FLOOR = 0.05 # never let combined-slip lateral capacity reach exactly zero RATIO_CLIP = 0.999 # keep Fx/D strictly inside the friction circle's smooth interior # ========================================================================== # Friction-circle combined-slip scaling # ========================================================================== def combined_slip_scaling(Fx_tire: float, D: float) -> float: """scaling = sqrt(max(0, 1 - (Fx_tire/D)^2)), clipped/floored for numerical safety at the friction-circle boundary (same treatment as every other physical floor in this series: a flat, zero- derivative region rather than an undefined one).""" ratio = Fx_tire / D ratio_c = max(-RATIO_CLIP, min(RATIO_CLIP, ratio)) scaling = math.sqrt(max(0.0, 1.0 - ratio_c ** 2)) return max(scaling, SCALING_FLOOR) def combined_slip_scaling_grad(Fx_tire: float, D: float, dD_dFz: float) -> Tuple[float, float, float]: """Returns (scaling, d(scaling)/d(Fx_tire), d(scaling)/dFz) -- the Fz-derivative chains through the given dD/dFz (from `peak_factor_and_derivative`). 12. What this is, and isn't

This closes a specific, precisely-measured gap with a guaranteed, architectural fix, validated against the actual previous headline findings to confirm nothing else moved. It is explicitly not:

A claim that symmetrized training data is useless. It's a real, if modest and inconsistent, improvement (Section 2) — just not sufficient on its own, and not what actually closes the gap.

Free. Two forward passes per evaluation instead of one is a real computational cost, unmeasured here in absolute terms but proportional and predictable — worth knowing before wrapping every tire-force call in every downstream deliverable's hot path.

A general claim that symmetrization improves accuracy. The slight RMSE improvement (Section 4) is a plausible, measured bonus for this specific model and dataset, not a guaranteed property of the technique.

Applied to any other component in this series. Only the tire model's lateral-force output is symmetrized. If a similar black-box-regressor-without-a-physical-constraint pattern exists elsewhere in the series (none currently identified), the same architectural-guarantee approach — rather than data augmentation alone — would be the lesson to carry forward.

This closes the last explicitly-open item from the risk-field integration deliverable's write-up. With this fixed, the tire-model lineage of this series (neural-Pacejka → bicycle dynamics → combined slip → risk-field integration → this fix) has no further flagged, unaddressed limitations.
